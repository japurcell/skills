# Agency priorities snapshot

Knowledge is limited to the self-contained skill snapshot checked **2026-10-20**. The USDA's available department strategy in that snapshot is the **FY 2026-2030 Strategic Plan Framework**; the full plan was still in development, and the FY 2022-2026 plan was listed under past plans.

## USDA's six goals

1. **Put American Farmers First.** Support farmers, ranchers, and producers across generations; improve access to production-agriculture technology; expand markets, trade promotion, and trade-data analysis.
2. **Prioritize Rural Communities and Economies.** Encourage private-sector engagement and innovation in rural economies, reduce living costs, develop American energy and natural-resource opportunities, and support aggressive wildfire response and proactive treatments.
3. **Protect and Expand the Resilience of American Agriculture.** Secure farmland, strengthen research security, and protect agriculture from animal and plant pests and diseases.
4. **Make America Healthy Again.** Address chronic disease through access to healthy, nutritious, real food, while maintaining a safe and affordable food system.
5. **Protect America’s Agricultural Heritage.** Protect producers from internal bureaucracy and support a regulatory environment conducive to agricultural production.
6. **Optimize USDA’s Customer Service and Protect the Taxpayers.** Improve efficiency and innovation to reduce waste, fraud, and abuse; strengthen service delivery through AI and advanced technology.

## Forest Service's eight priorities

1. **Employee and public safety:** put safety first in operations and maintain a culture of care.
2. **Wildfire readiness and response:** protect lives, communities, property, and resources through coordinated response, year-round capacity, community planning, recovery, and fuel treatments.
3. **Active forest management:** restore forest health, expand timber production and grazing, accelerate reforestation and reviews, and support wood-product markets.
4. **Minerals and energy:** enable responsible critical-mineral exploration and compatible energy development.
5. **Disaster recovery:** help communities recover, repair infrastructure, restore landscapes, and reduce future damage.
6. **Recreation access and visitor experience:** improve trails, campgrounds, facilities, roads, bridges, and recreation sites; strengthen visitor services and responsible recreation.
7. **Deregulation and organizational efficiency:** reduce unnecessary process burdens and administrative layers to improve delivery, accountability, and taxpayer value.
8. **Partnerships and shared stewardship:** coordinate with states, Tribes, local governments, and private landowners on forest health, wildfire resilience, recreation, conservation, and economic outcomes.

## FY26 context and FY27 status

Chief Tom Schultz's January 16, 2026 article, *Focus on the fundamentals: FY26 agency priorities*, reinforces those same eight Forest Service priorities. It emphasizes care and safety, year-round fire readiness, thinning and prescribed fire, healthy local mills, expanded grazing access, faster reforestation and reviews, responsible resource development, community recovery, modernized recreation infrastructure, fewer administrative layers, and state, Tribal, and local collaboration.

This snapshot is checked **2026-10-20**. The FY26 article and snapshot do not establish a FY27 successor or prove that no later direction exists. Therefore, final FY2027 priorities cannot be established from this knowledge.
