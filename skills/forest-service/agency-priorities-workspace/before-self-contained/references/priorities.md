## USDA department strategy

Start at [Our Performance](https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/general-information/our-performance). It owns the current strategic-plan link.

At the check date, the page said the full FY 2026-2030 Strategic Plan was in development and linked the **FY 2026-2030 Strategic Plan Framework**. It placed the FY 2022-2026 plan under past plans. The framework is the available current strategic framework, not a completed full strategic plan.

Retrieval note: the canonical host returned HTTP 403. The [USDA delivery endpoint](https://usda.azureedge.us/about-usda/general-information/our-performance) exposed the page and its [framework PDF](https://usda.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/documents/usda-fy-2026-2030-strategic-plan-framework.pdf). The linked one-page PDF was opened and read. Recheck the canonical page before relying on this alternate or assuming the framework remains the newest publication.

The framework's six numbered goals, paraphrased from PDF page 1:

1. Put producers first through sustained support, agricultural technology, and stronger market competitiveness.
2. Strengthen rural economies through private investment, lower living costs, energy and natural resources, and proactive wildfire treatment and response.
3. Build agricultural resilience by protecting farmland, research, and animal and plant health.
4. Improve health through nutritious food and a safe, affordable food system.
5. Protect agricultural heritage by reducing internal bureaucracy and improving the regulatory environment for production.
6. Improve customer service and taxpayer value through operational efficiency, reduced waste, fraud, and abuse, and AI and advanced technology.

For Forest Service alignment, goal 2 explicitly covers natural resources and wildland fire. Goal 6 addresses service technology. Whether a particular project contributes is an analytical judgment requiring project evidence.

## Forest Service agency priorities

Source: [Forest Service Priorities](https://www.fs.usda.gov/about-agency/priorities), retrieved directly on 2026-10-20. No publication date was established from the retrieved page. Its introductory theme is a return to fundamental work and measurable public value.

The page presents eight areas:

| Area (paraphrased) | Scope in the source |
| --- | --- |
| Employee and public safety | Integrate safety throughout operations and maintain a culture of care. |
| Wildfire readiness and response | Safe, aggressive initial response; year-round readiness; fuel treatments and prescribed fire; community protection. |
| Active forest management | Timber production, grazing access, forest health, reforestation, faster reviews, and wood-product innovation. |
| Energy and mineral resources | Responsible critical-mineral access, exploration, permitting, and compatible energy development. |
| Disaster recovery | Restore infrastructure, landscapes, forests, and affected communities. |
| Recreation access and service | Improve facilities, roads, trails, visitor engagement, and trip-planning services. |
| Efficient organization and processes | Reduce administrative burdens and layers, improve service delivery, and implement the USDA reorganization plan. |
| Shared stewardship | Work with states, Tribes, local governments, and private landowners across jurisdictional boundaries. |

Use the corresponding topic heading on the source page as the locator. This list is a summary, not a ranked allocation formula.

## FY26 leadership direction

Source: [Focus on the fundamentals: FY26 agency priorities](https://www.fs.usda.gov/inside-fs/leadership/focus-fundamentals-fy26-agency-priorities), Chief Tom Schultz, published January 16, 2026; retrieved directly on 2026-10-20.

The article reinforces the same eight areas above. Its topic sections emphasize safety, wildfire readiness, timber/grazing/reforestation, minerals and energy, disaster recovery, recreation infrastructure, organizational efficiency, and partnerships/cooperative federalism. It links readers to the agency priorities page.

Use this article to explain FY26 leadership emphasis. Its FY26 label does not establish FY27 direction. Neither its publication date nor the later retrieval date proves that no newer direction exists.
