# Agency Priorities

## Establish the applicable direction

1. Identify the user's agency scope and planning period. Distinguish USDA department goals, Forest Service agency priorities, and fiscal-year leadership direction.
2. Read [source notes](references/priorities.md) for the canonical links, dated baseline, and source-specific cautions. For current claims, open the applicable canonical sources:
   - **USDA:** Open the Our Performance page, follow its current strategic-plan PDF link, and read the PDF itself. Record its title, fiscal years, and whether it is a framework, draft, or full plan. A date range overlapping today does not make a plan current when USDA lists it under past plans.
   - **Forest Service:** Read the agency priorities page for agency-wide direction.
   - **Annual direction:** Read the FY26 leadership article for FY26 requests. For another fiscal year, find the corresponding official direction before asserting that the FY26 article still applies.
3. Record the retrieval date and publication date or fiscal-year scope for each source used. Read the relevant goals and objectives, not only search snippets. When sources differ, explain their dates, scope, and explicit replacement status; do not silently combine them into one official list.

If a source is inaccessible, try an official alternate or a user-supplied copy with attributable provenance. State exactly which source remains unverified. Use bundled notes only as a dated baseline, and label conclusions provisional when freshness cannot be checked. Never invent a newer plan, a fiscal-year update, or missing PDF contents.

## Apply the priorities

- For a briefing, separate department strategy from agency priorities and annual direction. Cite the PDF page or the web section supporting each group of claims.
- For a proposal or work-plan review, map each relevant activity to a sourced goal or priority, explain the causal connection, and identify evidence needed to demonstrate contribution. Label this mapping as analysis, not an agency endorsement.
- Keep unsupported alignment explicit. Do not force every project into every priority or invent targets, funding eligibility, mandates, or official performance measures.
- Treat proposed measures as suggestions unless the source establishes them. Priorities do not themselves grant project approval or replace applicable requirements.
- Answer unrelated requests normally without adding agency framing.

## Deliver

Match the requested format. Include the applicable period, source links with useful page or section locators, and any freshness limits. For alignment work, a compact table of activity, sourced priority, rationale, and evidence gap is usually sufficient.

Before finishing, check that every asserted official priority has a source, every interpretation is identifiable, and historical direction is not presented as current.
