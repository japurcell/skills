## Question

What happens when retrieval or maintenance fails, a session ends unexpectedly, or multiple agents change the same repository knowledge?

Define pending-work ownership, duplicate trigger handling, partial-write recovery, retries, cancellation, and visibility of incomplete maintenance. Establish a durable completion record and recovery behavior that meets the agreed lifecycle guarantees. Determine which failures block agent work and which permit continued work with an explicit outstanding obligation.

Preserve [Context Retrieval Contract](context-retrieval-contract.md): attempt focused recovery automatically, expose incomplete delivery, and pause only work dependent on missing policy or a material unresolved fact while independent work continues. Choose retry, recovery, publication, and concurrency mechanisms within that boundary; do not reopen it as a blanket task-stop policy.

Implement the verified-completion, visible-incomplete, pending-work, and next-eligible-event semantics in [Lifecycle Guarantees](lifecycle-guarantees.md). Respect explicit pauses/cancellation without restarting canceled user work, keep retries bounded, and do not equate provider termination with successful completion. Define how foreground stage callbacks and compatible skill/source-ingestion entries avoid recursive scheduling and duplicate semantic work. Choose ownership and records without weakening those agreed boundaries.

Implement [Context Organization and Skill Boundary](context-organization-and-skill-boundary.md): bind invocation context to workspace/worktree, task, agent, and stage; define issuance, validation, expiry, and restoration without trusting a provider-named environment variable. Invalid learn/dream context fails before mutation. Separate CLI dispatch/process success from validated completed, no-change, or incomplete stage outcomes. Choose actual outcome checks, publication/rollback, and durable ignored-state records. Preserve stable unit references and context availability when knowledge moves or changes during active, resumed, or delegated work, without detailed usage attribution.

Implement [Maintenance Scheduling and Pruning](maintenance-scheduling-and-pruning.md): distinguish the due review cycle, assigned required batch, reviewed coverage, and remaining routine work. Choose durable ownership and progress records across concurrent agents/worktrees, interruption, source revisions, and repeated native events. Verify scoped no-change without claiming full-cycle completion; only verified cycle completion resets its clock. Preserve pending obligations, unresolved candidates, active recovery records, and required evidence through cleanup. Completed unreferenced operational records expire after 30 days unless still needed for recovery or validation. Keep foreground execution and respect existing pause/cancellation rules; do not add idle model jobs.

## Resolution

The user confirms this contract on 2026-09-29 after accepting Q1-Q13, reviewing the concrete authority, duplicate-event, and optional-child explanations, and answering Q14 with "I confirm". This contract selects operational coordination and recovery; it performs no product implementation, installation, or provider certification.

### Coordination boundary and invocation validation

1. **Coordination boundary.** Use independent state and knowledge publication per worktree. Agents using the same worktree coordinate through one local publication authority; separate worktrees retain independent pending work and reconcile versioned knowledge through the normal version-control workflow. Do not automatically copy runtime obligations between worktrees. First-version writable knowledge roots must belong exclusively to one worktree; shared external mappings may be read-only. Shared writable roots across worktrees require a later explicitly supported coordination path.
2. **Invocation validation.** Use an opaque invocation handle issued by an enabled adapter at a supported lifecycle event. The CLI checks that handle against a persistent local registration bound to the repository/worktree, work session/task, executing agent, stage, and authorized scope. Caller-supplied identity flags or provider environment variables alone are insufficient. Invalid learn/dream invocation fails before mutation. This is an operational registration check, not proof of semantic work or a security boundary against another process with the same local access.

### Invocation lifetime, reentry, delegation, and operational state

3. **Invocation lifetime and restoration.** Use a finite, renewable registration for each active stage attempt. Completion, explicit pause/cancellation, expiry, or a changed worktree/agent/stage binding invalidates its mutation authority. Renew only through the enabled integration and checked CLI operations while the attempt remains eligible; an old handle cannot restore itself. Resume or compaction revalidates the executing agent and restores required context before issuing or renewing authority. Keep the durable obligation even when an attempt expires. Children receive their own scope-bound registrations. Concrete lifetime limits and provider-specific renewal evidence require Adoption and Validation certification.
4. **Duplicate events and reentry.** Use one durable obligation for the same work session, stage, and relevant input generation. Repeated provider events, compatibility skill entries, and source-ingestion gates join that obligation. An event cannot reuse a completed outcome after relevant inputs change. Coalesce new scope into pending work, or register a follow-up generation if the current attempt cannot safely include it. Stage-generated callbacks belong to the current lifecycle operation and do not recursively create a new learn/dream operation. Track genuinely new external work separately; do not suppress it as a duplicate.
5. **Delegated work.** The parent work session owns aggregate completion learning and its assigned routine dream batch. Children receive verified scoped context and their own invocation registrations, return relevant outcomes/evidence/candidates, and satisfy their assigned obligations before the parent can complete. A child stop alone does not schedule another full learn/dream pass. Urgent child repair or an independently registered work session may require separate work, coordinated within the same worktree. Missing verified child delivery or registration keeps that child path unsupported rather than assuming inheritance.
6. **Operational state.** Use one ignored SQLite database per worktree for invocation registrations, obligations, ownership, stage outcomes, and dream coverage. Keep transactions short and configure crash-durable local operation; expose human-readable status and JSON through the CLI. Check the installed Python's sqlite3 capability and filesystem assumptions during activation. No database server or third-party Python package is required; absent capability is an actionable unsupported-runtime error before mutation, not a silent weaker fallback. SQLite transactions do not include external Markdown publication; the journaled protocol below handles that publication and its recovery. Concrete runtime versions, settings, and platform checks belong to Adoption and Validation certification.

### Authority, duplicate-event, and child examples

These examples clarify operational permission, joined learning requests, and optional task children. They preserve the user's instruction authority and the foreground semantic workflow.

- **Invocation authority.** Mutation authority means an invocation's operational permission, not instruction authority. The enabled provider adapter registers or renews the attempt through checked integration events; agent-brain CLI enforces the stored identity, scope, status, and expiry. The foreground agent holds the handle and performs semantic work. These local components require no additional model executor. The user's instructions still govern what changes are allowed. Illustrative certified Copilot path: a completion event registers learn handle L1 for the active task/worktree/agent; CLI validates L1 before an allowed mutation; verified stage completion invalidates L1. If interrupted before completion, the obligation persists, expired L1 cannot mutate, and the next eligible event revalidates the task and issues a fresh attempt handle. This is a proposed flow, not deployed support.
- **Joined learning requests.** A provider completion event, update-agent-docs compatibility entry, and pending source-ingestion gate can request learning for the same work session and inputs. All join obligation L1; the foreground agent runs one coordinated learn pass, including required ingestion and affected-knowledge refresh. A verified result satisfies matching gates. A later relevant source change updates pending scope or creates follow-up work rather than reusing the stale result. Stage callbacks do not recursively start another maintenance pass.
- **Optional task children.** Children are optional task subagents created through supported, authorized delegation. Agent-brain does not create a maintenance agent or require children. For example, the main task agent may delegate hook checks and CLI-help review, give each verified assigned-scope context, gather their findings and fulfilled obligations, and then perform aggregate learn and its assigned due dream batch. Without delegation, the foreground task agent performs the same lifecycle alone. Unsupported child event/context paths remain unsupported.

### Stage ownership and observable outcomes

7. **Stage ownership.** Allow one active mutating learn/dream stage owner per worktree. Acquire an expiring exclusive claim in a short SQLite transaction and retain an incrementing ownership generation. Every mutating CLI operation verifies the current owner, generation, invocation validity, and scope. Reasoning does not hold a database transaction open. A valid existing owner retains its work; matching requests join it. After confirmed expiry or release, an eligible integration may acquire a fresh claim to recover the durable obligation; the stale owner cannot publish under the old generation. Explicitly paused/canceled work cannot be reclaimed as an active user task. Independent task work may continue, subject to the closed retrieval and completion contracts. Resolve unfinished publication state before beginning unrelated mutation. Publication follows the journaled protocol below. Concrete wait durations and tighter provider budgets remain for Adoption and Validation Contract.
8. **Observable outcome checks.** Use scope- and revision-bound completion records, with compact evidence proportional to the claim. The runtime records scoped input delivery, verifies referenced artifacts and required checks, and checks that the recorded outcome corresponds to the relevant inputs and actual changes. A no-change outcome needs evidence of the scoped review and required checks, not only an empty diff or an agent's statement. Reuse a result only while its relevant inputs remain applicable and unchanged. Pending required work, missing material evidence, incomplete delivery, or failed required checks keeps the applicable outcome incomplete. Keep receipts lightweight, without full transcripts or detailed usage attribution. The CLI can verify observable conditions; independent pilot checks assess semantic correctness under [Knowledge Evidence Policy](knowledge-evidence-policy.md) and [Success Criteria](success-criteria.md). Preserve the accepted distinction between completed review and verified facts, including uncertainty disposition rather than claiming inaccessible facts are verified. Concrete checks and test cases remain in Adoption and Validation Contract.

### Publication, references, and review progress

9. **Coherent publication and partial-write recovery.** Prepare a complete scoped change set outside canonical guidance; have the CLI validate it and recheck its base/input revisions. Persist recoverable before/after contents, affected identities, checks, and publication intent before changing canonical files. All agent-brain maintenance writes use the CLI's coordinated publication path. A bounded exclusive publication section prevents coordinated writers from interleaving; managed retrieval uses a verified coherent view or exposes unavailable affected context while publication/recovery is unfinished. Verify resulting files and references before accepting completion. Recovery compares current content with journaled versions: finish a still-valid prepared plan, or restore only recorded changes that can be safely reversed. Preserve unexpected edits and the recovery record when a conflict prevents either coherent outcome. Continue independent work under the closed affected-action rules. SQLite does not create a filesystem-wide atomic transaction, and unmanaged direct reads have no all-files-at-once visibility guarantee. Keep attributable reversible history; do not reset or commit unrelated user changes. Concrete filesystem durability and platform behavior need certification.
10. **Stable references and context freshness.** Bind delivery and review records to stable unit IDs, content/applicability revisions, required-reference revisions, and an executing-agent context generation. Preserve IDs through moves; repair required links and keep meaningful successor/retirement information for merges or removals. Never silently redirect an ID to guidance with different meaning or scope. Respect explicit whole-artifact reads and loading order. Publication and external-edit detection invalidate affected indexes and delivery/review records. Before newly dependent work, restore missing or stale relevant context; unrelated changes do not require rereading all guidance. Compaction/resume and child delivery revalidate availability rather than treating a past receipt as current context. Use integration-issued durable identities bound to the actual repository/worktree/task/agent; provider IDs are mappings only where verified. A changed workspace binding needs revalidation, not automatic inheritance or copying another worktree's obligations. Concrete schema fields remain for Adoption and Validation Contract.
11. **Dream cycle and batch progress.** Record a finite cycle target when review begins: unit/candidate identities, relevant input revisions, reviewed coverage, and pending dispositions. Each assigned batch receives its own identity and required scope. Credit only verified review/disposition of the applicable revisions; changes to target content or required inputs invalidate affected coverage and requeue it. Keep quiet-area rotation progressing. Queue later routine additions explicitly for subsequent coverage without losing them or pretending the completed target included them; urgent work retains its existing immediate timing. An individual batch's scoped no-change closes only that required batch. Mark a full cycle complete and reset its clock only when the recorded target is covered, its credits remain valid, and its required obligations are settled. Remaining unassigned routine coverage is distinct from an unfinished required batch. Precise batch limits and schedule initialization remain for Adoption and Validation Contract.

### Bounded recovery and retention-safe cleanup

12. **Bounded retries, visible failure, and cancellation.** Use a shared retry budget per stage obligation and eligible event, defaulting to at most three total automatic attempts, with at most one focused semantic repair. Repeated callbacks and compatibility entries cannot reset that budget; tighter provider continuation limits take precedence. Retry recoverable transient errors with capped waits and revalidate changed inputs before conflict repair. Invalid authorization, unsupported runtime capability, or unresolved policy does not receive blind same-input retries. After the budget is exhausted, report an explicit incomplete outcome, failed check/reason, affected scope, and next recovery opportunity in human-readable and structured status. Retain pending work; independent work can continue, but required incomplete work prevents successful work-session completion. The next eligible supported event rechecks access, context, and conditions before recovery. Explicit pause/cancellation revokes the active attempt, preserves attributable evidence and appropriate disposition, and never restarts the canceled user task. Recovery may perform authorized deterministic reconciliation of partial publication to restore a coherent state; it cannot silently resume canceled semantic work. Missing or corrupt expected operational state is recovery-needed, not proof of an empty queue or completed work: preserve recoverable records, rebuild only derived data, and expose unrecoverable missing input. Ask the user only about unresolved intent/policy or input that automatic recovery cannot obtain. Concrete wait durations, lower budgets, and provider mappings remain for Adoption and Validation; count all retry/repair work in accepted performance and usage measurements.
13. **Retention-safe cleanup.** Check active references and recovery requirements before transactionally retiring closed operational records that have reached the accepted 30-day unreferenced retention limit. Preserve pending obligations, unresolved candidates, active ownership/recovery/publication records, required evidence, and validation-pinned records. A verified no-change batch does not make the rest of its cycle disposable. Essential evidence and reversible history must remain portable with versioned knowledge rather than existing only in one ignored worktree database; preserve them before removing an operational record, or retain that record while still required. Remove resolved candidates from the active queue only after recording their disposition. Rebuildable caches can be regenerated, but cleanup must not treat durable state as cache. Do not delete the active database/identity container as ordinary record cleanup. Retain records longer when the accepted recovery or pilot requirements need them. No new age-based knowledge-pruning rule is introduced.
